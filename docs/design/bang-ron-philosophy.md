# Design philosophy: "Băng Rôn" (Matchday Tape)

A visual movement for football cards, written before any layout. Its subject
is the stadium on matchday: LED hoardings ringing the pitch, the turnstile
ticket torn in half, the stub kept in a pocket all evening.

Space and form: the canvas is a strip of hoarding, not a floating poster.
Content lives on full-bleed horizontal bands that run edge to edge like boards
around a pitch; nothing important ever floats on empty ground. Bands stack
from top to bottom in reading order — competition, confrontation, evidence,
stub — so the eye descends the way a match unfolds. Ticket devices do the
framing: a perforated tear above the provenance stub, semicircular notches
bitten from band edges, bordered stub boxes holding crests and numbers. The
work must look printed and torn, not rendered and placed, the product of a
print room that has made ten thousand match tickets.

Color and material: warm ticket paper, deep turnstile ink, one signal orange
burning like an LED row. Color is information and never decoration: ink bands
mean "read this first," orange means "this side won" or "look here once,"
paper means open ground. Nothing else on the canvas is allowed a strong
color, and no color ever appears as a floating strip behind text — color
always arrives as architecture the text stands on.

Scale and rhythm: type is set like hoarding lettering — one enormous
condensed voice, cropped without fear, filling its band to the edges. Numbers
are monuments in a distinct latin face because digits carry no diacritics and
deserve their own material. Everything else whispers from the stub in small
sentence-case microcopy. The rhythm is band, breath, band: dense information
alternating with strict hairlines, never with voids.

Composition and balance: asymmetry with intent, always left-anchored like
broadcast graphics stacking names over numbers. Borders are structural, never
ornamental: a stub box earns its border by holding identity, a band earns its
edge by separating meaning. Ornamental frames, centered symmetry, and
decorative dividers are what generic templates do when they have nothing to
say, and they are banned here.

Visual hierarchy through restraint: each card answers its questions with
exactly one device per question — who plays in stub boxes, how much in
monumental numerals, in which match in the LED band, proven by what in the
stub. Anything that does not answer is removed. The final artifact must look
labored over for hours by someone at the top of broadcast print design:
every band full-bleed, every tear aligned, every numeral grounded, nothing
floating, nothing accidental.
